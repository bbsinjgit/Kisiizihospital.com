Where the need is greatest

Six programmes doing the most difficult work.

Behind the department list are wards and outreach programmes built for the region’s hardest cases — the ones a rural referral hospital can’t choose not to run.

Neonatal

Special Care Baby Unit

Premature and critically ill newborns referred from government hospitals across the region are treated here with thermal support, phototherapy, CPAP and transfusions — kept running by the hospital’s own hydropower.

In 2024 the unit took in an abandoned newborn found in a forest — nursed back to health by the SCBU team, and later welcomed permanently into a family.

Mental health

The Ahumuza Centre

Ahumuza means “comfort.” Running since 1997, it offers inpatient, outpatient and community mental health care in a country with roughly one psychiatrist per million people.

60–70% of patients treated at the centre show measurable improvement in their mental health.

Maternity

Mothers’ Waiting Home

Low-risk expectant mothers travelling long distances can stay on-site ahead of labour, moving to the main ward only when needed — easing pressure on the maternity ward and improving safety for women arriving from afar.

Kisiizi partners with the Uganda Maternity & Newborn Hub and Royal Berkshire Hospital, and contributes to the international “WOMAN” obstetric trial.

End-of-life care

Palliative Care Service

A community-based service that takes end-of-life care out to patients’ homes, working alongside local leaders and Village Health Teams to meet the physical, spiritual and social needs of the dying — not just the medical ones.

Funded almost entirely by donation: free medicines, staff training and comfort items like blankets and clothing for families who have nothing left to give.

Menstrual health

The Empower Programme

A programme built to stop menstruation from ending a girl’s education. Local tailors — including single parents and people managing health challenges of their own — sew reusable pad packs, which are distributed in schools alongside menstrual health education.

Nearly 11,000 girls reached so far — each £6 pad pack lasts around three years, long enough to see a girl through to the next stage of school.

Nutrition

Nutrition Project

Children admitted with malnutrition are cared for on the ward, but the work doesn’t stop at discharge. The team follows up with community visits to each child’s home, alongside nutrition education and play therapy, and supports income-generating projects — chickens, dairy, fish and garden — that help families keep their children healthy for the long term.

A Church of Uganda mission hospital in the Kigezi highlands of Rukungiri District, South-Western Uganda.
